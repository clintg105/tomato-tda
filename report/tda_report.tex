\Abstract{
    Given a finite metric space \((X,d)\) induced by text encodings (BoW, TF–IDF, BERT) and metrics (Cosine, Minkowski, Sinkhorn–Wasserstein, Geodesic), we build Vietoris–Rips filtrations \(\mathrm{VR}(X;\varepsilon)\) and compute persistent homology in degrees \(n=0,1,2\). In total, we assess 54 embedding/metric combinations and perform topological data analysis (TDA) on 270 metric spaces.

\subsection{Metric Spaces}
To apply TDA, we require a notion of distance between points (reviews) in our feature space, since persistent homology typically operates on a metric space \cite{edelsbrunner2009computational}.
\subsubsection{Cosine Distance}
Given two vectors $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d$, we obtain their angle using the dot product $\langle x, y\rangle  = |x||y|\cos(\theta).$
This is converted to a distance via 
$$d_{\cos}(x,y) = \sqrt{2\left(1 - \cos(\theta)\right)} \in [0,2].$$
Cosine distance focuses on the difference in direction of the vectors, effectively comparing the relative term weights. For TF–IDF or BoW, this is useful because it reduces the influence of document length or overall word frequency. 
\subsubsection{Minkowski Distances}
A broad family of distances defined as 
$$d_p(x,y) = \Big(\sum_{i=1}^d |x_i - y_i|^p \Big)^{1/p}$$
for $p\ge1$ satisfy the metric axioms \cite{viro2008elementary}. We consider two important cases:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Manhattan distance} ($\ell_1$ norm, with $p=1$)
	$$d_{1}(x,y) = \sum_{i}|x_i - y_i|$$
	If $x,y$ are BoW vectors, $d_1$ counts the total difference in word counts between two reviews. This can be more robust than Euclidean ($p=2$) to high-dimensional sparse differences, since it doesn’t overly emphasize large single-component differences (instead, it treats each dimension linearly).
	\item \textbf{Chebyshev distance} ($\ell_{\infty}$ norm, the limit as $p \to \infty$)
	$$d_{\infty}(x,y) = \max_i |x_i - y_i|$$
	In text, $d_{\infty}$ might be interpreted as looking at the word with the most disproportionate frequency between two reviews. For example, if one review heavily uses the word “excellent” and another does not use it at all, Chebyshev distance would equal the normalized frequency difference for "excellent".
\end{itemize}

\section{Full Structure}
\subsubsection{Characterization}
On each normalized diagram we compute total persistance and persistant entropy,
\begin{align*}
	\operatorname{TP}_k(X)&=\sum_{\left(b_i, d_i\right) \in \operatorname{Dgm}_k(X)}\left(d_i-b_i\right)=\int_0^{\alpha_{\max }} \beta_k(\alpha) d \alpha\\
	\operatorname{E}_k(X)&=\sum_{\left(b_i, d_i\right) \in \operatorname{Dgm}_k(X)}-p_i\log(p_i), \quad p_i=\frac{d_i-b_i}{\sum_j\left(d_j-b_j\right)}
\end{align*}
capturing overall lifetime and dispersion of features \cite{adams2017persistence}.

The topological analysis of movie reviews written by Dennis Schwartz reveals intriguing structures in the BERT embedding space. As shown in Figure \ref{fig:schwartz_diagram}, Schwartz's reviews demonstrate a notable presence of higher-dimensional topological features, with 7 dimension-1 features (loops) and 3 dimension-2 features (voids). The maximum persistence value for dimension-1 features is 0.029, while for dimension-2 features it is 0.017, indicating reasonably stable topological structures.

This non-trivial topology suggests that Schwartz's critical language forms distinct clusters with interconnections, creating loop structures that likely represent recurring thematic elements or linguistic patterns in his reviews. The presence of these loops implies that Schwartz's critical style may orbit around certain conceptual centers before returning to common reference points or perspectives—a pattern consistent with a critic developing signature analytical frameworks.

Comparing the Betti curves in Figure \ref{fig:betti_curves}, we observe that Schwartz's reviews maintain higher persistence in dimension-1 features compared to Roger Ebert's reviews. This suggests that Schwartz may employ a more structured approach to criticism, with greater consistency in how he frames and develops his arguments across different films.

The dimension-2 features (voids) are particularly interesting, as they indicate three-dimensional "bubbles" in the semantic space. These voids might represent conceptual boundaries in Schwartz's criticism—areas where he deliberately avoids certain types of analysis or language. Alternatively, they could reflect a tendency to approach films from multiple contrasting perspectives, creating a more spherical structure in semantic space as his analysis encircles the subject from different angles.

From a stylistic perspective, these topological features may correlate with Schwartz's writing style, which tends to balance academic analysis with accessible criticism. The presence of both loops and voids suggests a critic who returns to foundational principles while maintaining conceptual distinctions between different types of films or critical approaches.

This sharp increase indicates a threshold at which many loops simultaneously form in the embedding space, suggesting a critical distance at which Schwartz's language patterns suddenly connect to create circular semantic structures. This abrupt topological transition might correspond to a characteristic scale at which Schwartz's critical vocabulary forms conceptual relationships—perhaps indicating a distinctive granularity at which his analytical framework operates most coherently. The spike's height and narrowness suggest these structures form rapidly and in large numbers at this specific scale, potentially revealing a fundamental frequency in his linguistic patterns or conceptual organization that distinguishes his critical voice.

The lower H1 count suggests that Ebert's language patterns create fewer circular structures in the semantic space, potentially reflecting a more accessible writing style that progresses linearly through observations without returning to previous points as frequently.

The comparative Betti curves further highlight that Ebert's dimension-1 homology features appear later in the filtration process and persist for shorter durations. These topological differences align with the widely observed stylistic contrast between the two critics: Ebert's more populist, accessible approach versus Schwartz's more academically-oriented criticism. The topological signatures thus provide a mathematical foundation for quantifying differences in critical voice that have traditionally been described in subjective terms.

\subsubsection{Observation 4: Content Rating (PG vs. R)}
The topological analysis of reviews categorized by content rating (PG vs. R) reveals subtle but consistent differences in linguistic structure. Using BERT embeddings with cosine distance, we observe a lifetime ratio of 1.11 for dimension-1 features, indicating that reviews of PG-rated films form more persistent loops in the semantic space compared to R-rated films.

However, for dimension-2 features, we observe an inverse pattern with a lifetime ratio of 0.89, suggesting that reviews of R-rated films develop more complex three-dimensional structures. This inverse relationship between dimensions 1 and 2 may reflect a fundamental difference in how critics approach films with different content ratings: PG reviews may emphasize interconnected themes (creating loops), while R reviews may explore more mature themes that create boundary delineations in the semantic space (forming voids).

The maximum persistence difference of 0.020 for dimension-1 features indicates that the most significant loops in PG reviews are more stable than those in R reviews. This could reflect how critics of family-friendly films might emphasize recurring themes and values, creating more stable circular structures in the semantic space.

These content rating differences persist across encoding methods but are most pronounced with BERT embeddings, suggesting that contextual encodings better capture the subtle semantic differences in how critics approach films with different target audiences.

\subsection{Preferred Metric}

To quantitatively compare the effectiveness of different distance metrics and encoding methods, we developed a topological complexity measure that captures both the quantity and quality of topological features. Our complexity formula is defined as:

\begin{equation}
C = \sum_{d=0}^{2} (n_d \cdot p_d \cdot w_d)
\end{equation}

where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $n_d$ is the number of features in dimension $d$ (count of points in the persistence diagram)
    \item $p_d$ is the total persistence of features in dimension $d$ (sum of all lifetimes)
    \item $w_d = d + 1$ is a dimensional weight that prioritizes higher-dimensional features
\end{itemize}

This weighted approach serves several purposes in our analysis. First, the feature count $n_d$ rewards metrics that can detect more distinct topological structures. Second, the total persistence $p_d$ emphasizes the stability and significance of these features, giving preference to metrics that identify longer-lived structures rather than noise. Finally, the dimensional weight $w_d$ reflects our understanding that higher-dimensional features (loops and voids) often capture more complex and meaningful semantic relationships than simple connected components.

This complexity measure allows us to systematically compare different combinations of encoding methods and distance metrics, identifying which approaches best capture the topological essence of movie reviews across different categorizations.

Our comprehensive analysis of various distance metrics across different encoding methods reveals clear patterns in their effectiveness for topological data analysis of movie reviews. As shown in Figure \ref{fig:complexity_heatmap} and Table \ref{tab:best-metrics}, certain combinations of encodings and metrics consistently outperform others in discriminating between meaningful categories.

BERT embeddings combined with the Minkowski \(p=\infty\) (maximum) metric demonstrated superior performance for detecting dimension-1 homology features, with discriminability scores of 158.5, 160.7, and 191.7 for drama/comedy, review type, and critic name categories, respectively. This suggests that the maximum difference between any pair of components in the BERT embedding vectors captures essential semantic structures that distinguish writing styles and content categories.

For dimension-0 features (connected components), we observed varied optimal metrics. The cosine distance with BoW encodings performed best for drama/comedy discrimination (9.3), while TF-IDF with Minkowski \(p=1\) (Manhattan distance) performed best for critic name discrimination (39.5). This variance suggests that different distance metrics capture different aspects of the document structure, with term frequency-based approaches better at identifying authorship patterns.

Across all datasets and metrics, the bottleneck distance between different critics' persistence diagrams revealed consistent topological differences. As shown in the critic topological comparison results, for BERT encodings with cosine distance, the H1 bottleneck distance was 0.031, while for Minkowski \(p=1\), it increased dramatically to 23.59. This substantial difference demonstrates that the choice of ground metric significantly impacts the topological features detected.

The Wasserstein distance consistently provided more nuanced discrimination between categories than the bottleneck distance, capturing gradual changes in the persistence diagrams rather than just the maximum differences. This was particularly evident in sentiment analysis, where the Wasserstein distance better captured the higher complexity of Fresh reviews compared to Rotten ones.

For applications specifically focused on detecting thematic loops in text (dimension-1 features), our results strongly favor BERT embeddings with either cosine or Minkowski \(p=\infty\) metrics, with the latter providing approximately 23\% better discrimination on average. However, for capturing broad semantic groupings (dimension-0 features), TF-IDF with Manhattan distance provided more reliable results.

In conclusion, while no single metric is optimal for all analytical purposes, BERT embeddings with Minkowski \(p=\infty\) demonstrated the strongest overall performance, particularly when analyzing the full dimensional structure (dimensions 0, 1, and 2 combined). The substantially higher discriminability scores (171.9, 173.8, and 347.6 for the three main categories) suggest that this combination captures the most meaningful topological structures in the semantic space of movie reviews.
